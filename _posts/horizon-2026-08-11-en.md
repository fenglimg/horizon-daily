The project is notable as a rare and technically significant security research contribution, though the supplied source material does not include detailed exploit mechanics or validation results. The item received meaningful community attention on Hacker News, with 112 points and 35 comments. Exact technical impact and practical exploitability remain unconfirmed from the provided information.

rss · Hacker News 首页 · Aug 10, 16:03

**「Background」** System Management Mode \(SMM\) is a highly privileged, invisible execution environment on x86 CPUs, typically used for firmware operations; it is triggered by a System Management Interrupt \(SMI\). This proof-of-concept exploits SMM by combining an exceptionally long-running instruction with a second core: one core is held outside SMM by the long instruction in a tight loop on a very slow load, while the other core enters SMM, creating a race condition that breaks the expected isolation. The technique highlights that SMM&\#x27;s assumption that all cores are quiescent between instructions can be violated, potentially leading to firmware-level compromise.

**「Impact」** This research demonstrates a method to compromise System Management Mode \(SMM\), a highly privileged x86 firmware environment that operates independently of the operating system, which could enable stealthy firmware-level attacks that bypass OS security functions. If weaponized, such an exploit could give attackers persistent control over affected systems, as SMM code has historically been used to circumvent operating system security and has been linked to critical vulnerabilities like CVE-2024-36311.

### [Berkeley MLS-Bench Shows AI Agents Optimize, Not Discover Methods](https://www.bestblogs.dev/article/82adc79ca0?utm_source=rss&amp;utm_medium=feed&amp;utm_campaign=resources&amp;entry=rss_article_item) ⭐️ 7.0/10

A detailed analysis of Berkeley&\#x27;s MLS-Bench benchmark argues that current AI agents excel at improving and combining existing components but fail at discovering novel, transferable research methods, challenging self-evolving AI claims. MLS-Bench spans 12 machine learning subfields and 140 real research tasks, requiring reproduction of at least three strong human baselines, restricting which components agents may modify, and validating transfer under at least three test conditions. Even after multiple iterations with strong baseline code, models tended to recombine and tune known modules rather than propose new mechanisms, and free compute-allocation experiments did not reliably improve research decisions. The piece places this in the AlphaGo-to-AlphaEvolve trajectory and says the scarce resource is compute that yields useful information, not candidate volume. rss · BestBlogs.dev · Aug 10, 16:00

**「Background」** MLS-Bench is a benchmark introduced by researchers at UC Berkeley and Princeton to evaluate whether AI systems can invent generalizable, scalable machine-learning methods rather than merely combine or apply existing techniques. It spans 140 research tasks across 12 ML domains and requires reproducing strong human baselines, constraining which components can be modified, and checking transfer across at least three test conditions. The creators report that current frontier agents remain far from reliably surpassing strong human-designed methods.

**「Impact」** For ML researchers and AI-agent developers, MLS-Bench provides an evaluation baseline that separates genuine method-level contributions from hyperparameter tuning and engineering, undercutting premature self-evolving AI claims and directing effort toward evidence-driven experiment planning.
