### [Octopus: History-Free Gradient Orthogonalization for Continual Learning \(CVPR 2026\)](https://www.bestblogs.dev/article/4aeced9db7?utm_source=rss&amp;utm_medium=feed&amp;utm_campaign=resources&amp;entry=rss_article_item) ⭐️ 7.0/10

Researchers from vivo BlueImage Lab and Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced Octopus, a continual learning framework for multimodal large language models that addresses catastrophic forgetting without storing historical data. The core innovation is HiFGO \(History-Free Gradient Orthogonalization\), which computes gradients of previous task parameters on current task data \(GPWC\) to approximate old-task sensitive directions and enforce orthogonal parameter updates. A two-stage fine-tuning strategy first learns new knowledge without constraints, then applies HiFGO constraints to consolidate, balancing plasticity and stability. On the UCIT benchmark, Octopus achieves state-of-the-art results, surpassing prior methods by 2.14% on average performance \(Avg\) and 6.82% on final performance \(Last\), while also achieving a rare positive backward transfer \(BWT=+0.41\). The method requires no historical data storage, adds no inference overhead, and shows robustness to hyperparameters and task order, making it suitable for edge or resource-constrained scenarios.

rss · BestBlogs.dev · Aug 19, 12:00

**「Background」** Continual learning for multimodal large language models faces the challenge of catastrophic forgetting, where learning new tasks degrades performance on previously learned ones. Traditional approaches include replay methods that store and reuse historical data, and regularization methods that constrain parameter updates, but both have limitations: replay requires memory and raises privacy concerns, while existing orthogonalization techniques often need access to old data or cause negative backward transfer. The UCIT benchmark is a standard evaluation for multimodal incremental learning, measuring both average performance across tasks and final performance after all tasks are learned.

**「Impact」** Octopus provides a practical solution for continual learning in resource-constrained environments, as it eliminates the need for historical data storage and adds no inference overhead, potentially enabling on-device fine-tuning of multimodal models. The positive backward transfer \(BWT=+0.41\) suggests the method can improve old task performance through new task learning, a significant advantage over traditional orthogonalization approaches that typically cause negative transfer \(e.g., BWT=-2.51\).
